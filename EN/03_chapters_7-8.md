**The Reason for the Cosmic Scale: Why Cells Become Colossal**

An understanding that a dense, vibrating medium inevitably self-organizes into stable cells with optimized pressure distribution raises the next fundamental question: why must these structures be colossal? What prevents aol space from fracturing into billions of small, local domains?

It is precisely at the junctions of these cells that unavoidable pressure imbalances, head-on collisions of differing vibrational modes, local stresses, and continuous energy losses due to the forced rearrangement of aols emerge. In other words, maintaining boundaries is far too costly for a dynamic system.

In physics, there is a pervasive principle governing the most diverse scales of matter: nature always strives to minimize the number of internal boundaries in order to reduce overall tension. We observe this in the macro-world when soap bubbles or mercury droplets merge into larger volumes, as well as in the micro-world where crystal grains and magnetic domains coarsen, pushing conflict zones outward. 

For an infinite aol space, the choice between a countless multitude of small cells and a small number of large ones is obvious. Coarsening radically reduces the total surface area of inter-cell boundaries, curtails zones of destructive conflict, and maximizes the internal volume featuring an ideally stable, coordinated mode of vibration.

Because the aol medium lacks conventional external constraints such as the solid walls of a vessel, the process of mechanical coarsening can proceed incredibly far. It continues until the system reaches a state of global dynamic equilibrium, at which point the benefit of further growth diminishes, oscillations are distributed optimally, and neighboring domains mutually balance one another. It is precisely for this reason that the natural scale of the cells proves to be neither room-sized nor even planetary, but colossal and cosmic.

At the same time, the Universe does not merge into a single, solitary giant cell for one simple reason: an infinite vibrating medium is physically incapable of synchronizing and maintaining a single, rigid vibrational mode across unlimited distances. Over immense expanses, autonomous, independent regions of coordination inevitably emerge. As a result, aol space takes on the form of a stable cosmic network composed of vast, adjacent cells, each representing a Mini-Universe with its own internal pressure gradient.
