## Chapter 7. AOLIC SPACE AND THE STRUCTURE OF SMALL UNIVERSES

Having introduced aols as the primary elements of the world, we must now take the next logical step and examine the medium as a continuous whole, rather than focusing on a single, isolated particle. In and of themselves, individual aols cannot yet explain the overarching structure of the Universe. 

They merely provide us with the "substance of space." Our task now is to understand how this substance is organized—why it exists under immense pressure, why it undergoes continuous vibration, and why specific regions of the cosmos foster the conditions for the birth of matter while others do not.

This inquiry brings us to one of the most vital paradigms of the entire aol model: the concept of the Universe as an interconnected system of Mini-Universes—colossal cells of aol space, each governed by its own autonomous internal mechanics.

**1. Inadequacy of the Concept of a Structureless, Infinite Medium**

The simplest hypothesis, often employed by traditional ether theories, postulates that the cosmos is an absolutely homogeneous, infinite, and chaotic array of contiguous particles. However, this view reaches an impasse upon the very first attempt to explain the nature of universal pressure from the perspective of short-range interaction.

If the primordial material medium were infinite and entirely devoid of internal structure, an absolute static equilibrium would be established within a very short time. Within a structureless, isotropic monolith, it is impossible to justify the emergence of stable pressure gradients, variations in physical conditions across different cosmic regions, or the very tendency toward the concentration of forces. An infinite medium without cellular boundaries would inevitably become dead, motionless, and incapable of development.

For the existence of perpetual dynamics, directed flows of momentum, and the continuous movement of matter, a more complex, ordered macro-organization of space is fundamentally required. This is precisely why aol physics asserts that the infinite Universe is structurally segmented into colossal, interconnected, and autonomous blocks — Mini-Universes.

**2. The Physical Nature of the Mini-Universe and the Law of Self-Organization**

The conventional conception of the cosmos as an infinite void in which sparse galaxies and stars are chaotically scattered is deeply erroneous. Chaos at the macro level is merely an illusion caused by the limitations of human perception. Dynamic matter fundamentally strives for equilibrium, which is achieved exclusively through rigid structural ordering.

Self-organization (synergetics) is a basic, inherent property of moving matter. Under the influence of continuous contact collisions, the elements of the medium inevitably rearrange themselves in such a way as to minimize the local tension of internal processes and arrive at the most stable, mechanically favorable state.  
In official physics, this law is described as the principle of energy minimization; in the aol model, however, its essence is expressed more precisely: as a tendency toward the minimization of local motion and pressure.

Examples of such self-organization in continuous media are well known in terrestrial hydrodynamics. A classic example is Rayleigh – Bénard convection cells. When a thin layer of liquid is heated uniformly from below, chaotic convection currents initially arise within it.
However, as the thermal load increases, the system rearranges itself, forming a strict grid of stable hexagonal cells. The physical motion within the medium does not cease; it becomes ordered, because the system finds the optimal spatial form for its existence at a given energy level.

A similar synergetic principle of self-organization operates on the scale of the entire cosmos. Because aol space is hyper-reactive and in continuous contact, absolute static rest within it is impossible. Striving for the most stable dynamic configuration, the gigantic universal array of aols naturally segments into an ordered system of macroscopic cells — Mini-Universes.

The name "Mini-Universe" should not be misleading; it denotes not a physically small scale, but rather its status as a structural building block of the cosmos. Each such cell represents a colossal region of space spanning billions of light-years. It is termed "Mini" solely because it does not constitute the entirety of the infinite Universe, but is merely one local unit within the boundless architecture of the cosmos.

**3. Criteria for Selecting the Geometrical Form of the Cell and the Rhombic Dodecahedron**

Mini-Universes cannot possess an arbitrary geometry, as three fundamental geometric and physical requirements are imposed upon the shape of a spatial cell. 

**First**, it must achieve seamless packing, meaning the figures must tightly fill three-dimensional space without gaps, overlaps, or spaces, entirely eliminating the occurrence of absolute void at the boundaries. 

**Second**, it must maintain dynamic contact, ensuring each cell touches its neighbors in such a way that a stable zone of mutual force balancing and minimal background pressure forms along their shared borders. 

**Third**, it must facilitate a uniform gradient, requiring a shape that provides a natural and isotropic escalation of pressure from the periphery toward the geometric center. Most classical stereometric figures fail to meet these criteria. 

Spheres are ideal for the isotropic transmission of pressure to a center, yet they are fundamentally incapable of filling three-dimensional space without leaving voids at their junctions. 

Cubes provide seamless packing, but due to their sharp vertices and edges, they are incompatible with the concept of uniform central compression.

In aol physics, the unique and natural solution to this problem is the rhombic dodecahedron — a twelve-sided polyhedron with identical rhombic faces. The rhombic dodecahedron stands as a unique volumetric figure capable of executing a tight, gapless packing of three-dimensional space. 

These cells represent a direct, volumetric analogue to the flat hexagons observed in Bénard convection cells. Such a spatial structure is supremely stable, as it allows the system to distribute internal vibrational pressure in the most uniform manner possible.
It is vital to understand that one does not need to assume the medium inherently "knew geometry." 

A stable geometry is born naturally out of the primordial chaos of vibrational pressure regions. This process acts as a mechanical analogue to biological evolution. Configurations incapable of correctly distributing the tensions of the medium are rapidly destroyed or restructured. 

Only those forms that best mitigate internal pressure imbalances survive, endure, and begin to repeat on a massive scale. Thus, the natural selection of forms leaves only the most stable structures within the Universe, and this is precisely how the rhombic dodecahedrons emerged.

Consequently, the Universe is not a collection of random objects, but a singular, self-organizing system in which form, motion, and pressure are deeply intertwined — a system that continuously sustains itself through its own mechanics.

**4. Mechanics of Peripheral Pressure and Cumulative Concentration of Forces Toward the Cell Center**

The starting point for the entire subsequent vibrational mechanics of the cosmos is the initial pressure at the boundaries separating the spatial cells. Because the infinite Universe represents a dense, self-organized system of contacting rhombic dodecahedrons, absolute void is completely absent between the faces of adjacent Mini-Universes. 

In a constant quest for dynamic equilibrium, the hyper-reactive array of aols continuously exchanges vibrations. Supporting one another along their flat boundaries, the Mini-Universes accumulate a mutual force vectors at their junctions. These force vectors compensate for one another, resulting in the lowest possible background pressure for this system being established at the cell's periphery. It is precisely this physical support that enables the generation of the initial baseline pressure. 

One must understand that the peripheries are not merely boundary lines or flat interfacial planes, but rather gigantic regions of space extending for billions of kilometers deep into the interior of the cells.

However, as one advances from the periphery toward the geometric center of the cell, the situation changes fundamentally. The mechanism of pressure escalation is driven by the geometric contraction of the surface area of concentric layers of matter. The vectors of vibrational pressure from the outer boundaries of the cell are transmitted through a chain of continuous collisions deep into the interior of the rhombic dodecahedron. 

At the outermost levels, this total aggregate force is distributed among a colossal number of primary elements. Yet, as the transmission progresses inward, the surface area of each subsequent internal spherical layer of aols inevitably decreases, and consequently, the number of primordial elements constituting that layer is reduced.

An internal mechanical effect of load redistribution arises within the cell, governed by the rule: "the external majority exerts pressure upon the internal minority." A broader outer layer of aols translates its cumulative momentum onto the adjacent inner layer, which consists of fewer particles. 

As a result, each individual aol of the inner level experiences a progressively increasing share of the total pressure of the surrounding medium. This process continues cumulatively all the way to the geometric center of the rhombic dodecahedron. Each deeper layer absorbs the compounding load from all the overlying, highly populated layers of primary matter, naturally transforming the core of the Mini-Universe into a zone of extreme, peak compression and maximum vibration intensity. 

By analogy with the concept of the periphery, it must be understood that the center of a Mini-Universe is a giant region spanning billions of kilometers, rather than a singular, dimensionless geometric point at the center of the rhombic dodecahedron.

**5. The Nature of Universal Micro-Vibration and the Three Factors of Medium Destabilization**

It would be unnatural to assume that the primordial medium of nature exists in a perfectly flawless packing, where every individual aol possesses the maximum number of contacts and the entire structure remains infinitely symmetrical. While such states are convenient for geometry, they are rarely encountered in the real world. 

Natural systems generally form not as idealized blueprints, but as living structures characterized by local deviations, structural imbalances, and a statistical distribution of elements. Therefore, it is far more reasonable to assume that aol space represents not an ideal crystal, but a dense yet incompletely ordered close-packing of aols. 

One aol may have 4 contacts with its neighbors, another 7, a third 10, and some up to 12. Consequently, pressure is distributed unevenly; in certain areas, an aol is compressed more tightly, while in others, it experiences weaker forces. Elsewhere, micro-backlashes occur, and contacts can be nearly direct or aligned at various angles. It is precisely this kind of medium that is capable of possessing internal dynamics, continuous rearrangements, and vibrations.

For this reason, aol space is fundamentally incapable of existing as a static, frozen monolith. Microcosmic vibrations are not an external supplement to pressure — they inevitably flow from it. Within any ultra-dense material medium operating under colossal mechanical tension, the continuous striving to equalize forces combined with the absolute impossibility of achieving total rest invariably generates self-sustained oscillations. 

At its fundamental level, the Universe is permeated by a universal, pervasive background hum. This vibrating array of aols never settles into a state of calm, not only due to the chaotic packing of the particles themselves, but also because the system is continuously driven by three fundamental factors that locally and globally destabilize the structure.

**The first factor** is the emergence of luonols. An initial spherical aol is perfectly integrated into the geometry of the medium, setting aside the chaotic nature of individual contacts. In contrast, a luonol represents a spatially distorted element.   
Its diametric recesses, or cavities, act as geometric defects that locally disrupt the uniform distribution of forces and the symmetry of the surrounding particles' packing. This plastic deformation of the primordial matter makes the medium fundamentally non-ideal. While the reader is not yet familiar with luonols, they will be introduced in detail within the subsequent chapter.

**The second factor** involves the presence of material objects. Any structures built from luonols and aols — ranging from solitary atoms to colossal planets and stars — constitute macroscopic disruptions in the homogeneity of space. They redistribute the flows of elastic collisions, generate local imbalances, and screen forces.   
The larger a material object is, the more severely it deforms the pressure gradient of the surrounding medium. Furthermore, these objects function as aggregators of oscillations, locally escalating the frequency of vibrations.

**The third factor** is the mechanical flattening of luonols at the peripheries of the cells. This process manifests with the highest intensity along the borders of the Mini-Universes. As cosmic objects migrate toward the cell boundaries and disintegrate due to a deficiency in pressure, the luonols that compose them are released from their atomic configurations. Finding itself face-to-face with the free aol medium, the deformed particle undergoes a mechanical processing of colossal proportions — a flattening or reshaping back into a sphere.

The essence of the flattening process is purely impact-driven and high-frequency in nature. The surrounding spherical aols continuously rush into the recesses of the liberated luonol. However, the background pressure at the periphery is insufficient to retain them inside the cavity. 

The spheres that enter the recess are instantaneously ejected by subsequent ricochets, as aols collide with the edges of the cavities and transmit recoil momentum back into the medium. Each such microscopic impact along the edge of the recesses, or within the cavity followed by an immediate ejection, generates a local high-frequency shock, while simultaneously flattening the luonols and restoring their spherical shape. 

Because the mass of the primordial matter being liberated at the outskirts of the cells is colossal, this flow of micro-interactions never ceases for a single moment. The space along the boundaries of the Mini-Universes literally "boils" with billions of elastic micro-collisions occurring at a tremendous frequency on the order of 10<sup>13</sup> Hz.   
The local perturbations from the reshaping luonols accumulate cumulatively and are transmitted through chains of contacting aols back toward the center of the cell, forming the continuous, pervasive vibrational background of the Universe.

**6. The Global Cosmic Conveyor and the Nature of Atomic Vibrations**

The described processes culminate in an infinite, continuously self-regenerating, three-stage cosmological cycle. 

**The first stage** consists of the birth of matter within the central regions of the Mini-Universes. If the pressure of the medium naturally escalates toward the center, then the central domains of the Mini-Universes inevitably become distinct zones of extreme physical conditions. 

Within these colossal spatial volumes, which are entirely baseline-incommensurate with conventional human scales, the external pressure of the medium reaches critical, peak values. It is vital to emphasize that this process does not unfold within an abstract mathematical point of singularity, but rather encompasses giant, real expanses.

It is precisely here, under the influence of monstrous compression, that the fundamental mechanism of aol deformation is triggered. This process does not occur ubiquitously or chaotically, but strictly locally — only where the mechanical load overcomes a specific structural threshold of strength.

Within these extreme central zones, conditions arise under which ordinary aols begin to deform irreversibly, acquiring recesses and transforming into luonols. Prior to this moment, as long as the spatial pressure remains moderate, the aols retain their original smooth, spherical shape. 

In this state, they are capable only of translating vibrations and transmitting pressure, but are fundamentally unable to form mechanical interlocks with one another. Only the ultimate compression at the cores of the Mini-Universes forces the primary elements of the medium to alter their geometry. The emergence of the recesses fundamentally changes the rules of the game, unlocking the physical possibility for the assembly of the first stable LEGO-like structures: atoms, molecules, and all subsequent tangible matter.

From this rigorous mechanics, it logically follows that the matter familiar to us does not exist eternally in a ready-made and immutable form. It is a product of the evolution of space itself, born exclusively within the dedicated factories of the aol medium where the necessary conditions have been established.

Thus, matter is generated not by a mystical prime mover, but by the vibrating medium itself, which, through the sheer force of its own pressure, forges stable geometric elements out of spherical aols.

**The second stage** involves the movement from the center to the periphery. The pressure gradient, which reaches its maximum at the center and decreases toward the outskirts, generates a constant centrifugal force, causing any complex material object to inevitably experience a vector expulsion outward. 

Newly formed matter does not remain forever in the central domain, as the vibrating medium itself continues to exert a continuous and strictly directed influence upon it. The escalation of spatial density toward the center creates a steady tendency of extrusion for all already established structures. 

Matter is literally pushed out from the zone of maximum compression into regions with a lower density of aols. This natural centrifugal drift of objects acts as the basic macroscopic "current" of the Universe, which will subsequently play a key role in explaining large-scale cosmic movements and governing galactic dynamics. 

Thus, the Mini-Universe functions as a cyclic dynamic system in which the medium first concentrates pressure toward the center to assemble material elements, and then systematically evacuates the results of this assembly to its periphery.

**The Third Stage: Disintegration of Matter at the Periphery and Completion of the Cosmic Cycle**

Within the peripheral sectors of the Mini-Universe cells, a physical process unfolds that operates in a manner directly inverse to the central assembly. As centrifugal displacement forces cosmic objects away from the center toward the marginal zones of low pressure, the external compression of the medium drops beneath a critical threshold, rendering it insufficient to maintain complex material structures in a stable configuration. Within these rarified domains, conditions materialize for the inevitable weakening, degradation, and disintegration of matter. 

Intricate atomic LEGO-like architectures fracture, liberating the constituent luonols. Exposed directly to the unconfined aol medium, these deformed particles undergo a massive mechanical restructuring — a flattening driven by the baseline vibration of the medium at a sharp frequency of 10<sup>120</sup> Hz, which irreversibly restores them to their pristine state of free, perfectly smooth, spherical aols.

Consequently, the Mini-Universe as a collective entity functions as a colossal, closed mechanical loop. Its core serves as a furnace of peak pressure where matter is forged, its intermediate zone accommodates the existence and directed drift of this matter, and its periphery facilitates its attenuation, decay, and reabsorption back into the continuous spatial medium. 

This fundamental concept of elemental recycling serves as the cornerstone for the cosmology of the aol model, a paradigm that completely replaces one-off, mystical origin scenarios such as a Big Bang erupting from a dimensionless mathematical singularity. 

Matter is fundamentally incapable of accumulating structural defects indefinitely, as it is cyclically returned to its primordial, idealized configuration. From this dynamic, an essential deduction emerges: atoms, molecules, planets, and stars are not eternal formations. 

Although they preserve stability for billions of years, each individual atom follows a strictly delineated life path spanning from its initial mechanical stamping at the core of the rhombic dodecahedron to its ultimate disassembly along the cell boundaries. Complex architectures merely secure a temporary lease on the primary elements provided by space.

The spherical aols themselves, however, are eternal. It is precisely this eternity of the aols, combined with the continuous emergence and flattening of luonols, that renders aol space a living, constantly vibrating medium that never subsides into total rest. Equilibrium within it is impossible not because it is unattainable in principle, but because it is continuously disrupted by the very nature of the ongoing physical processes. 

This disruption serves as the ultimate source of all oscillations observed throughout the Universe, establishing aol space as the eternal, primary Mover of all cosmic dynamics. The scale of the background micro-vibrations within this medium is colossal. 

While human perception is adapted to a "slow" macro-world, official science has experimentally confirmed that the thermal oscillations of a substance’s crystal lattice, and even the so-called "zero-point oscillations" of atoms at absolute zero temperature, occur at a frequency on the order of 10<sup>120</sup> Hz. 

Official physics reaches an impasse when attempting to explain the source of this perpetual, undampened activity of an isolated atom in the complete absence of heat. In aol physics, the answer is strictly mechanistic: atoms do not oscillate on their own due to some mythical internal energy. They are submerged within a hyper-reactive medium and continuously absorb its elastic collisions. The trembling of atoms is a direct consequence and a precise indicator of the continuous micro-vibration of aol space itself.

**The Reason for the Cosmic Scale: Why Cells Become Colossal**

An understanding that a dense, vibrating medium inevitably self-organizes into stable cells with optimized pressure distribution raises the next fundamental question: why must these structures be colossal? What prevents aol space from fracturing into billions of small, local domains?

The answer lies in the mechanical price the medium pays for every internal boundary. A multitude of small cells implies a colossal total surface area of the borders separating them. Any boundary within a continuous medium represents a zone of fundamental imperfection. 

It is precisely at the junctions of these cells that unavoidable pressure imbalances, head-on collisions of differing vibrational modes, local stresses, and continuous energy losses due to the forced rearrangement of aols emerge. In other words, maintaining boundaries is far too costly for a dynamic system.

In physics, there is a pervasive principle governing the most diverse scales of matter: nature always strives to minimize the number of internal boundaries in order to reduce overall tension. We observe this in the macro-world when soap bubbles or mercury droplets merge into larger volumes, as well as in the micro-world where crystal grains and magnetic domains coarsen, pushing conflict zones outward. 

For an infinite aol space, the choice between a countless multitude of small cells and a small number of large ones is obvious. Coarsening radically reduces the total surface area of inter-cell boundaries, curtails zones of destructive conflict, and maximizes the internal volume featuring an ideally stable, coordinated mode of vibration.

Because the aol medium lacks conventional external constraints such as the solid walls of a vessel, the process of mechanical coarsening can proceed incredibly far. It continues until the system reaches a state of global dynamic equilibrium, at which point the benefit of further growth diminishes, oscillations are distributed optimally, and neighboring domains mutually balance one another. It is precisely for this reason that the natural scale of the cells proves to be neither room-sized nor even planetary, but colossal and cosmic.

At the same time, the Universe does not merge into a single, solitary giant cell for one simple reason: an infinite vibrating medium is physically incapable of synchronizing and maintaining a single, rigid vibrational mode across unlimited distances. Over immense expanses, autonomous, independent regions of coordination inevitably emerge. As a result, aol space takes on the form of a stable cosmic network composed of vast, adjacent cells, each representing a Mini-Universe with its own internal pressure gradient.

**Chapter Summary**

The Universe is neither an infinite void with rare, isolated celestial bodies, nor is it a dead, endlessly homogeneous slurry. The cosmos is a boundless, self-organized network of Mini-Universes shaped as rhombic dodecahedrons. 

Firmly contacting one another along their faces, these gigantic building blocks generate a baseline background pressure at their borders. As this pressure is translated through chains of rigid spheres toward the interior of the cells — and cumulatively compounded by the shrinking surface area of concentric layers — it progressively escalates toward the geometric centers.

Consequently, aol space constitutes a continuous, ultra-dense, high-frequency vibrating medium existing under gradient pressure. It is precisely the dynamics and geometry of such a hyper-reactive primordial medium that enable the birth of stable matter, the contact-driven movement of bodies, and absolutely every physical phenomenon of our reality — transforming light, heat, electricity, inertia, and gravity into different operational modes of a singular, unified aol space.
