## Chapter 4. THE FOUR KINDS OF MECHANICS

There are four types of mechanics.  
Three of them are known to science, but the fourth is not. It is precisely due to this ignorance that physicists invent absurdities that they themselves do not understand ("Shut up and calculate!"). They have immersed themselves in mathematics; there is no physics left there.

Yet, all that was required after the first three steps was to take the final, fourth logical step in the exact same direction. Everything would have fallen into place. There would have been no need to fabricate the incredible, justifying it by claiming that the invisible world allegedly operates under its own, inadequate laws.

Let us list the four types of mechanics. 

**1. Classical Mechanics — The Mechanics of Solid Bodies**

You encounter it every single second; you live within it. You are incapable of breaking away from it for even a brief moment. In this mechanics, solid objects make contact and act upon one another. You see it, you feel it, you do it, and you understand it perfectly. This interaction always takes the form of either contact pressure or a contact impact (which is simply short-term pressure). In other words, the principles of pressed—moved and press—it moves are at work here. 

All mechanisms and machines are engineered based on these very principles. Everything created by humans has been made through contact pressure. Look around you; recall your day. In the visible and tangible physical world of solid bodies, you have never once encountered anything other than contact pressure.

It would never cross anyone's mind to explain the movement of furniture by the influence of abstract "fields," quanta of massless energy, or an inherent desire of the wardrobe itself to move.
Because that would be ridiculous.  
In this mechanics, everything is obvious.

**2. Fluid Mechanics**

It is commonly referred to as hydraulics. Here, too, everything is logical—molecules of a liquid make contact and exert pressure upon one another or upon an actuating mechanism, such as a piston. It is the exact same principle: pressed—moved. Nothing else exists, nor can it exist. It is pure mechanics, albeit wet.  
The mechanics of an isolated solid body have been replaced by the mechanics of a continuous medium, yet the physical essence of the process—contact, the transfer of pressure, and the resulting motion—remains unchanged.

**3. Gas Mechanics**

This is pneumatics. Under normal conditions, a gaseous medium is completely invisible to the human eye; however, it obeys the very same unshakeable laws of the mechanics of contact collisions.  
Air can move objects.  
It can destroy structures.  
It can lift airplanes.  

By virtue of what? By virtue of contact pressure.  
It is simply that the particles are now further apart from one another, and their motions are more chaotic, but the principle remains identical: contact and the pressure of gas molecules upon one another and upon actuating mechanisms. It is the exact same principle: pressed—moved. Pure mechanics—already invisible to the eye, yet still comprehended by the mind.

Into none of these three types of mechanics can one insert fantasies about other kinds of interactions. Everywhere, there is only contact and pressure; everywhere, there is only a single mechanism of interaction: pressed—moved. Without contact and pressure, nothing would ever transpire. Reflect upon these first three types of mechanics, and you will easily realize this.

The world of solid bodies, the world of liquids, and the world of gases operate solely under the laws of mechanics. Denying this is impossible.

Why, then, did no one assume that nature would not behave bizarrely with processes invisible to the eye in the micro-world, and that the same logical and natural principle—pressed—moved—would operate there as well?

Why have physicists so convoluted the intrinsic simplicity of nature, attributing absurd interactions like action-at-a-distance attraction and action-at-a-distance repulsion to the invisible world?

Furthermore, science is utterly incapable of describing these mechanisms. It is incapable for an obvious reason: it is impossible to describe that which does not and cannot exist. For as long as humanity has existed, attraction has remained an absolute mystery.

Why should everything end with three types of mechanics?  
Why are we expected to believe that:  
• At the level of solid bodies, mechanics is at work;  
• At the level of liquids, it is also mechanics;  
• At the level of gases, it is mechanics yet again.

And then, suddenly:  
• Abstract fields without a carrier appear;  
• Energy emerges as a "something" capable of pushing entire galaxies;  
• Attraction occurs without contact.

Why? Because we cannot see it? But we cannot see gas molecules either. That does not prevent them from operating according to mechanical laws.

Therefore, let us speak of the fourth type of mechanics.  
The fourth type of mechanics is just as natural and logical, and exactly identical to all the others.

**4. Aol Mechanics**

This mechanics manifests in the micro-world, gravity, and motion (in what is commonly called "inertia"). Its operating principle is exactly the same: contact-pressed and moved. Nothing else can exist in nature. It is incapable of interacting in any other way.

For motion to occur, contact and pressure are required.  
The world of solid bodies, liquids, and gases is screaming this at us. Hear this scream.  

If the very same mechanical principle operates across all levels accessible to us, then it is this very principle that is fundamental to the entire Universe.

Space is not empty — it represents a super-dense medium composed of discrete elements that continuously interact with one another and transmit mechanical pressure. They are the true quanta of this world.

The aol medium serves as the single foundation for all known physical processes.  
The aol space carries light as a purely mechanical wave of collisions, enables electrical and magnetic processes through the dynamics of the medium, presses bodies toward one another (gravity) via an external pressure gradient, and sustains inertial motion where the classical viewpoint fails to see any active forces.

Thus, the fourth mechanics is not an isolated branch of physics, but the very primal foundation of all the others (given that the motion of "inertia" plays a colossal role in any motion within both the macro- and micro-world).

In reality, there is no multitude of fundamentally distinct "types of interaction" (strong, weak, electromagnetic, gravitational). There is only one single, universal mechanism that manifests itself identically across all states of matter: in solid bodies, liquids, gases, and within the most fundamental medium itself.

The entire Universe obeys the rigid rule of the fundamental law of physics: press — it moves.  
This approach eliminates the chaos currently entrenched in science. The pileup of scattered, abstract rules is replaced by a through-and-through, monolithic logic.

**Nature is unified.** It did not invent special laws just for what humans are incapable of seeing. Human vision is far too weak a tool, and nature is under no obligation to adapt to its limitations. The invisibility of a medium does not prove its absence. Poor eyesight is a human problem, not nature's.

Nature is logical and pragmatic. It does not pervert itself with wild fantasies. It has no need for them when it has an excellently functioning principle right at hand—a simple principle of contact pressure that works always and everywhere, with all objects: solid, liquid, gaseous, immense, and microscopic.

Do you possess enough intellect and logic to comprehend these simplest four types of mechanics?




## Chapter 5. SPACE IS NOT EMPTY. THE DRIVING MEDIUM

We have already taken several steps.
We have seen that motion does not arise by itself. That it requires pressure. That this principle operates in solids, in liquids, and in gases. And that it is reasonable to expect it to continue at a deeper level.

But now a sharper question emerges.
All right — pressure produces motion.

But what produces pressure itself?
What is the source of all these effects?
If this question is not asked, the picture remains incomplete. We see the consequences, but we do not understand the cause.

Let us once again begin with something simple.
You push an object. In this case, everything is clear: you create the pressure. But you did not appear out of nowhere either. Your motion is caused by the work of your muscles; the work of your muscles is the result of processes in your body; and those processes, in turn, depend on chemical reactions, nutrition, and the surrounding environment.

If we continue this chain, it becomes clear: no motion arises “on its own.” It is always connected to another motion.
One process initiates another.
One form of motion is transmitted further.

And if we do not artificially break this chain, a very important conclusion follows.
**Matter can be moved only by other moving matter.**

This may sound almost like a tautology, but it is precisely such statements that often turn out to be fundamental.
No “abstract force” comes from outside. No “energy” starts a process by itself. There is always a specific motion that is being transmitted further.

This can be seen in any example.
A hammer strikes a nail — the motion of the hammer is transferred to the nail.

A flow of water turns a turbine — the motion of the water is transferred to the blades.

The wind pushes a sail — the motion of the air is transferred to the ship.

It is always the same.
Not “something acts,” but something moves and transmits that motion further.

And here we reach the boundary of everyday experience.
Because at some point, a question arises: **what sets the entire world in motion?**

Not a single object, not a mechanism, not a flow of water—but everything at once.
What creates pressure at a level where there are no “hands,” no “pumps,” no “wind”?
If space is truly empty, as it is commonly assumed, there is no answer.
There are ideas that people become so accustomed to that they stop questioning them.
One of these ideas is emptiness.

From childhood, it is easy for us to imagine that between objects there can be “nothing.” Empty space. A complete absence of matter. A pure vacuum. Something like a vast transparent stage, on which stars, planets, atoms, photons, and everything else are later arranged.
It is a very convenient picture.

But what is convenient is not necessarily true.
If we begin to examine it step by step, it turns out that the idea of emptiness is associated with too many difficulties. And the most important of them is this: emptiness can do nothing.

It cannot exert pressure.

It cannot transmit motion.

It cannot serve as a support.

It cannot participate in any process.

This is not philosophy. It is pure mechanics.

Let us begin with the simplest possible question.
Imagine that you have two objects. Between them — emptiness. One object must somehow influence the other. How would this occur?
If there is truly nothing between them, then there is nothing through which the interaction can be transmitted.

One might say: “through a field.” But this immediately raises the next question:
What is a field?
If it really acts, then it must be something. It must have a carrier. It must consist of something, or at least be transmitted through something. Otherwise, we have simply replaced one mystery with another.

To say that “the interaction is transmitted through a field” is not yet an explanation. It is only a new name for an unknown mechanism.

Now let us look at an even simpler case.
Consider the motion of a body.
If a body is truly in absolute emptiness, then what does it push against when it begins to move? What provides resistance to the start of its motion? And what would continue to sustain its motion once the initial push has ended?

If there is no medium between the body and the rest of the world, then the very idea of interaction becomes strange. It would mean that an object either moves by itself, or resists within itself, without any support. But this contradicts the most basic experience of everyday life.

To exert pressure, there must be a support.
To transmit pressure, there must be contact.
For contact to exist, there must be a medium — or at least a chain of material elements.
Emptiness provides none of these.

And here a crucial shift occurs.
If motion exists — and it does — if interaction exists — and it does — if the transmission of motion exists — and it does — then there must be something material between objects through which all of this takes place.

Otherwise, the world becomes a collection of miracles that we are simply asked to accept.
At this point, we can take the next step and state it directly: **space is not empty.**

It is filled.
Not with air, not with gas, not with any familiar forms of matter, but with a deeper medium—a medium that exists everywhere. A medium in which all objects are immersed, from the scale of atoms to that of galaxies.

In this book, this medium is called aolic space.
It is important to avoid a possible misunderstanding from the very beginning.
When I speak of a “medium,” one should not imagine something like a fog or a liquid in which objects float like fish in water. That would be far too crude an image.

We are dealing with a medium that is far denser, more fundamental, and more universal.
It does not merely “fill the gaps.”
It is what we usually call space itself.
In other words, space is not an empty container in which objects are placed.
Space itself is matter.
Simply matter of a different level.

At this point, a natural question may arise: if space is filled with some kind of medium, why do we not notice it?

The answer is very simple.
For the same reason that a fish does not perceive water as something separate—until there is a current, pressure, or resistance. We do not live next to this medium; we live within it. We ourselves are part of a world that is built in it and out of it.

Moreover, a human being does not perceive everything that exists, but only what affects the senses in a form accessible to them. Gas was once invisible and not obvious. Electricity as well. Magnetic interaction even more so. The absence of direct perception does not mean the absence of the medium itself.

In some cases, the opposite is true: precisely because the medium is everywhere, it ceases to be perceived as something separate and begins to feel like a “natural background.” And then a further mistake is made—the background is declared to be emptiness.

Now let us consider what such a medium must be like, if all processes truly occur through it.

First, it must be continuous. Otherwise, gaps and empty intervals would reappear—regions through which no interaction could be transmitted.

Second, it must be capable of exerting pressure. Otherwise, it would be unable to move anything.

Third, it must be capable of vibration. Otherwise, it would be impossible to account for light, heat, oscillations, and much more.

Fourth, it must be sufficiently dense to serve as a support for motion, yet sufficiently subtle and universal to permeate everything.

This is precisely what aolic space is.
Without going into detail for now, it can be imagined as a medium composed of an enormous number of identical primary elements. Later, we will call them aols and examine their properties in detail. For now, the key idea is simple: there is no “nothing” between objects.
There is a material medium between objects.

And this idea changes almost everything.
It immediately becomes clearer how the transmission of motion is even possible.
If space is filled, then any impulse does not vanish into emptiness, but is transmitted further through the medium.
It becomes clearer how pressure can exist without visible contact between bodies.
If there is a medium between them, then interaction can propagate through it, step by step.

It becomes clearer how light, electrical processes, magnetic vortices, and gravitational pressing can exist.
In all these cases, there is no longer a need to invent different kinds of entities. It is enough to recognize that between all bodies there is always the same mediator — aolic space.

And here it is important to emphasize one more point.
This medium is not passive.

It does not merely “exist.”

It acts.

It exerts pressure.

It vibrates.

It transmits motion.

It forms structures.

It breaks structures apart.

It sustains processes.

In other words, aolic space is not a backdrop to the world.
It is its primary participant.

And here we arrive at a thought that will have to be repeated many times, because it is truly central: **the main driver of all processes in the universe is aolic space.**

Not energy.

Not a field.

Not attraction.

Not an abstract force.

But the medium itself — continuously pressing and continuously vibrating.

It creates objects.

It holds objects together as objects.

It restructures matter.

It transmits motion from one object to another.

In this sense, everything we observe in the world is a different form of behavior of one and the same material foundation.
Once this idea is accepted, everything else begins to reorganize.

Light can no longer be explained as “flying photons.”

Electricity can no longer be explained as “moving charges.”

Gravitation can no longer be explained as “attraction.”

Inertia can no longer be explained as a property of a body to “keep moving by itself.”

In every case, the question must be asked: how does the medium behave?
What pressure does it create?
How does it transmit motion?
And it is precisely this approach that makes physics mechanical—and understandable—again.

Let us summarize.
Emptiness is too weak an assumption for the real world. It explains nothing, because it can do nothing.

If motion exists, if interaction exists, if the transmission of motion exists, then space cannot be nothing. It must be something.

In this model, that “something” is aolic space — a continuous, dense, material medium that fills the universe and participates in all processes.

In the next chapter, we will take the next step.
We will try to understand what this medium is made of.
Because if space is matter, then it must have its own elements.
And if that is so, it is time to become acquainted with them.




## Chapter 6. AOLS — THE PRIMARY ELEMENTS OF THE WORLD

If we have already taken the step of recognizing that space is not empty, but is instead a real material medium, then a natural question follows: what is this medium made of?
It is not enough to say, “there is some kind of medium,” and leave it at that. If it is material, if it transmits motion, if it exerts pressure, vibrates, and participates in all processes, then it must have its own elements.

This is where a concept appears in the aolic model that will be fundamental for the entire book: **aols.**

The word itself may seem unfamiliar, but there is nothing unusual about that. Any new model of the world inevitably introduces its own basic terms. At one time, words like “atom,” “electron,” and “quark” played that role. Here, that role is taken by the word aol.

But of course, the important thing is not the word itself, but what stands behind it.
An aol is not “just another particle” among those already known. In this model, an aol is a primary element of the world—the deepest level of matter we reach in our reasoning.

In other words, we are not speaking about a particle that makes up some particular thing.
We are speaking about a particle that makes up everything.

Not only bodies.

Not only atoms.

But space itself.

At this point, it is necessary to state clearly what properties these elements must have, if they are to form the continuous medium of the world.

**First of all, aols must be identical.**

Why is this important?
Because if, at the most fundamental level, there were many different kinds of primary elements, the world would be inherently irregular and chaotic. 
It would then be difficult to explain why the same laws operate in widely different regions of the universe. Why the transmission of motion follows the same principles everywhere. Why the properties of space remain consistent.
Thus, the first essential property of aols is their uniformity.

This does not mean that they are merely similar.
It means that they are truly identical as the basic elements of the medium.

**The next property is their shape.**
In the aolic model, aols are assumed to have a **spherical form.**
This is not an arbitrary assumption. A sphere is the most natural form for an element that must interact equally in all directions. A sphere has no preferred direction. No edges that could lead to accidental engagement. No protrusions that would make one direction different from another.
If the primary elements of the medium are to form a universal space, then spherical symmetry is the most consistent assumption.
But spherical form alone is not sufficient.

**Aols must be extremely hard.**
Why?
Because it is through them that extremely high-speed interactions must be transmitted. Light, vibrations, pressure — all of these require a medium capable of transmitting effects quickly and reliably.
If aols were soft, they would damp motion. Transmission would be slow and viscous. But the world shows us that there are processes in which effects propagate at extremely high speeds. Therefore, the elements of the medium must possess a corresponding degree of rigidity.

However, there is an important subtlety here.
**Aols are not infinitely rigid.**
This must be emphasized.
If they were absolutely undeformable, then the transmission of interaction through them would occur instantaneously, rather than at a finite speed. But we observe that the propagation of light and other effects occurs at a finite speed. Therefore, aols must possess a very high, yet still finite, rigidity.

It is precisely this that makes elastic transmission possible: an aol responds to an impact and passes it on further. It does not “lag” significantly, but neither does it transmit the effect instantaneously.

**Another fundamental property of aols is the absence of friction in the usual sense.**
It is easy to make a mistake here if friction is understood too superficially.
In the familiar macroscopic world, friction arises from the interlocking of surface irregularities, from the embedding of one surface into another, from the tearing of protrusions, from the deformation of contact points. But if the primary elements of the world are perfectly spherical and extremely rigid, such mechanisms simply do not exist between them.

Aols do not catch on one another, because there are no protrusions.
They do not scratch one another, because there is no roughness.
They do not become lodged in one another, because they are extremely rigid.

As a result, under ordinary conditions, they can move relative to one another without the kind of friction that is characteristic of the macroscopic world.

And this is crucial. Otherwise, the medium itself would be “viscous” in the worst sense of the word and would slow down all processes.

**Aols are eternal.** They do not consist of parts or layers. They are a single, unified form of matter that cannot be broken into pieces, and that is not subject to wear or erosion. One can wear down something that consists of parts — but an aol has no parts; it is a single whole.

At this point, the reader may ask: if aols are so smooth, so identical, so spherical — how can a complex world arise from them? How do atoms, molecules, bodies, and substances emerge from perfectly smooth spheres? 

This is an excellent question.
The answer will be given later, when we examine the deformation of aols and the formation of lunols. For now, it is important to understand that at the most basic level, aols are simple—and that is their strength. They are not required to contain the entire diversity of the world within themselves. Their role is to serve as a universal foundation.

Diversity appears later, when, under immense pressure, some of them begin to change their shape.
But for now, we are dealing with the initial level — with aols as the “zero layer” of matter.
There is one more important property.

Within this model, aols must be indivisible.
Not because this is convenient, but because otherwise we would never reach a true foundation. If every explanation can be indefinitely postponed to “an even deeper level,” the model will never be complete. Therefore, in this framework, the aol is understood as the limit of the divisibility of matter.

In other words, an aol is that from which everything else is composed — but it is not itself treated as a complex structure.

Now we come to a very important point, one that often вызывает внутреннее сопротивление—one that often meets with inner resistance.

If aols fill all of space, does that mean that there is absolutely nothing between them?
The answer here is subtle.
When spheres are packed closely together, minimal geometric gaps inevitably arise. This is easy to understand even from simple examples like balls: no matter how tightly they are pressed together, tiny spaces remain between them, dictated by geometry itself.

In the aolic model, only this kind of “emptiness” is allowed—geometrically unavoidable, minimal, confined between contacting spherical elements.
But this is not the kind of emptiness that is usually meant when physics speaks of a vacuum or empty space.

Such a geometric gap does not form an independent region of the world. It is immediately “controlled” by the surrounding dense medium. It cannot become a place where something happens independently of matter.

In simple terms — absolute emptiness does not exist.
There are only minimal gaps, unavoidable in the dense packing of spheres.
And that is a crucial difference.

Now let us bring everything together into a single, vivid picture.
Imagine not an empty universe in which objects drift somewhere within it.
But a universe completely filled with an immense number of the smallest identical spherical elements.

These elements are in contact.

They transmit pressure to one another.

They receive and transmit vibrations.

They form the very fabric of space.

In such a picture, space ceases to be an empty stage.
It becomes a dense mechanical reality.
Aols, in this framework, are not “components inside objects.”
**They are the universal matter of the world.**
Both bodies and what appears to be the “space” between them are built from them.

And here another important idea emerges.
If aols constitute the very medium of space, then it is their properties that determine the properties of the world.

Why is the speed of light finite?
Because aols possess finite rigidity.

Why can space transmit pressure?
Because aols are in contact and press against one another.

Why is emptiness impossible?
Because all of space is filled with aols.

Why is mechanics possible at all levels?
Because the foundation of the world consists of elements in contact.

In other words, aols are not merely a “hypothetical particle.”
They are the key to explaining everything that follows.

However, another question arises.
If aols are so smooth and so identical, what causes them not only to transmit motion, but, under certain conditions, to become the basis of real, stable structures — atoms, molecules, and matter?

Answering this question requires the next step.
We must understand how the aolic medium behaves as a whole. How pressure is distributed within it. Why regions of immense compression arise. And why it is precisely in these regions that some aols may change.

This is where we will go next.
Because knowing the “building blocks” alone is not enough.

We must also understand what forces — and under what conditions — cause these building blocks to become structures.
And that is the subject of the next chapter: aolic space as a continuous, vibrating, pressure-exerting medium of the universe.
