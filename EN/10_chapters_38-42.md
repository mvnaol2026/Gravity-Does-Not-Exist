## Chapter 39. MAGNET. INTERACTION OF MAGNETS

A magnet is one of the most familiar and at the same time one of the least explained objects in standard physics. Everyone has held it in their hands. Everyone has felt how it attracts iron and how two magnets repel each other.

But what exactly happens inside a magnet, and how does it act through space?
There is no clear mechanical explanation.
In the aol model, the mechanism is explained.

**What Happens Inside a Magnet**

Let us start with atoms. Atoms of different substances have different structures — and different abilities for the rotation of their parts. Iron atoms have a special feature: their parts are capable of rotating along the axis of the magnet’s body — longitudinally. This is a fundamental difference from atoms of copper or aluminum, where rotation is only transverse.

In an ordinary piece of iron, these atoms are oriented chaotically — in all directions. Their longitudinal rotations are directed differently and cancel each other out. There is no net effect.

But when iron becomes magnetized — under the influence of an external magnetic field or an electric current — the atoms gradually align in one direction. Their longitudinal rotations become coordinated.

And at that moment, something qualitatively new arises.
The coordinated rotating parts of the atoms begin to drive the aols of space in one direction — along the magnet. The aols move through the body of the magnet from one end to the other, exit outward, encounter the aol space, flow around the magnet externally, and return back. A closed, stable circulation of aols is formed.

This is a permanent magnet. 
Not an object with two poles, but an object within which a continuous closed flow of aols of space exists.

**What Are Poles**

The north pole is the place where aols exit the magnet outward.
The south pole is where they re-enter back inside.
These are not two separate parts of the magnet. They are the inlet and outlet of a single “pump”.

**Why Can You Not Cut Off a Magnetic Pole**

There is a simple experiment that confounds intuition. Take a magnet and cut it in half — expecting to obtain a separate north pole and a separate south pole. But each half immediately becomes a complete magnet with its own north and south poles. Cut it again — the same result. No matter how many times you divide it, it is impossible to obtain a “pure” pole.

The reason is that poles are misunderstood from the very beginning. They are perceived as parts of the magnet — as if north and south were two separate elements that could be detached. But this is not the case. Poles are not structural components. They are manifestations of a single closed process.

In the aol model, a magnet is not an object with two poles, but an object within which a stable, ordered motion of the aol space exists. The atoms of the magnet, due to their ability for longitudinal rotation, organize aols into directed flows — outward from the north pole, along the outside of the magnet toward the south pole, inward through the south pole, and back through the body of the magnet to the north pole. This is a closed circulation. It cannot have a beginning or an end — because the aol medium is continuous, and pressure within it does not allow discontinuities. Any break would be instantly compensated by a reconfiguration of the medium.

When a magnet is cut, the closed system of motion is disrupted. But the aol space immediately restructures — and in each fragment a new closed structure forms. Cutting does not destroy the mechanism of magnetization. It merely changes the geometry — and the system instantly adapts, creating two new closed loops instead of one.

Obtaining a monopole — a magnet with only one pole — is impossible not because “nature is built that way” in some abstract sense. It is because closed motion in a continuous medium cannot be broken. 

You cannot cut off the inlet of a pump from its outlet and leave only the inlet — the pump would simply cease to be a pump. The same applies to a magnet: cutting off one pole would mean breaking the closed process. But the medium does not allow this — it immediately closes the loop again.

**Magnetic Field Around a Magnet**

What is called the magnetic field is precisely this flow of aols outside the magnet. Aols exit from the north pole, spread into space, flow around the magnet, and enter the south pole. This is why iron filings scattered around a magnet arrange themselves into characteristic curved lines — they simply show the direction of aol motion in space.

This field is not created out of nothing. It does not appear instantly from nowhere. It is a redistribution of already existing aols of space — the same ones that fill everything around us. The magnet does not produce new matter. It organizes what already exists.

**Why Does a Magnet Attract Iron**

When a piece of iron enters the flow of aols created by a magnet, this flow begins to act on the iron’s atoms — causing their parts to rotate in one direction. The atoms of iron begin to align in a coordinated way, meaning they become temporarily magnetized. And now we have two objects with coordinated aol flows — the magnet and the magnetized iron.

Between them, a region forms where aols move in a coordinated way, and the pressure is lower than outside. The aol space outside presses more strongly — and presses the iron toward the magnet. It does not attract. It presses.

**Why Do Two Magnets Repel with Like Poles**

When two north poles approach each other, both magnets push aols toward one another. The two flows collide head-on. The pressure between the magnets sharply increases — higher than outside. The aol space between the magnets pushes them apart.

When a north and a south pole approach, the situation is reversed. The north pole of one magnet pushes aols out — the south pole of the other receives them. The flows do not collide but align — one continues the other. The pressure between the magnets drops below the external pressure. The aol space outside presses them together.

There is no attraction. There is a difference in pressure.

**Why Heating and Impact Demagnetize a Magnet**

A permanent magnet appears stable and almost eternal. It retains its properties for years. But if it is strongly heated or sharply struck, its magnetic properties weaken or disappear completely.

Why?
A magnet is not a thing with poles. It is a state of matter. Inside it, atoms are oriented in a coordinated way — their rotating parts all point in the same direction, collectively organizing the aols of space into a single ordered flow. As long as this coordination is preserved, the magnet exists. Once it is disrupted, the magnet disappears as a phenomenon, although the material itself remains.

Heating is an increase in vibrations. The aol space begins to shake the atoms more intensely. Up to a certain temperature, the system holds — internal bonds are strong enough to maintain coordination despite increased vibrations. But when the critical temperature — the Curie point — is reached, chaos overcomes order. Atoms begin to deviate from the coordinated direction, turn randomly, and the unified structure collapses. The magnetic state disappears.

Impact acts differently — not gradually, but instantly. A sharp mechanical force creates a wave of displacements within the material. Atoms that were aligned become abruptly shifted from their positions. Coordination is disrupted — the structure collapses. Even if the magnet appears intact externally, its internal structure is already disordered.

Both effects do the same thing through different mechanisms. Heating destroys order through accumulated vibrational chaos. Impact destroys it through sudden displacement. The result is the same — coordinated motion turns into chaotic motion, and the magnet ceases to be a magnet.

**How Magnetization Occurs**

If a magnet can be demagnetized, then it can also be created. How does an ordinary piece of iron become a magnet?

The answer begins with the fact that iron already contains everything necessary for magnetism. Its atoms are capable of longitudinal rotation — precisely the property that allows organizing the aols of space into directed flows. But in the normal state, these atoms are oriented chaotically. Their individual contributions cancel each other out, and there is no net magnetic field. Iron can be a magnet, but it is not one due to the lack of coordination.

To create a magnet, a directing influence must be introduced — bringing another magnet close, passing an electric current, or placing the iron in an external magnetic field. This influence creates an ordered motion of the aol space within the material — directed vortices and flows of aols that begin to act on the atoms of iron.

The atoms begin to rotate. Not all at once — the process is gradual. First, separate regions appear where atoms become aligned. These regions grow, capture neighboring atoms, and merge. When a sufficiently large part of the structure becomes coordinated, a single closed loop of aol motion forms, and the material becomes a magnet.

Once the structure is established, it becomes stable on its own. Atoms in the coordinated state support each other — each is held by the pressure of the aol space and the geometry of neighboring atoms. Therefore, even after removing the external influence, the magnet retains its state. This is a permanent magnet.

Magnetization is not the addition of something new. It is the reorganization of what already exists.

A chaotic structure becomes coordinated. And now the full picture becomes clear: magnetization and demagnetization are the same process in two directions — the creation of order and its destruction. 
A magnet exists as long as coordination exists. 
And it disappears when that coordination is destroyed.

**Main Conclusion**

A permanent magnet is not a mysterious object endowed with mystical attraction. It is a piece of matter in which atoms, in a coordinated way, organize a closed flow of aols of space. This flow creates a pressure difference around the magnet — and it is precisely this pressure difference that is responsible for everything we call magnetic phenomena.

No action at a distance.

No matter created from nothing. 

Only the motion of aols — contact and pressure.




## Chapter 41. ETHER

Aol space could be called ether. I deliberately do not do this.
The word “ether” carries a long trail of incorrect ideas. In the minds of most people, including physicists, it is something ethereal, vague, almost mythical. A subtle, weightless substance barely present between bodies — no more than a poetic image, convenient for talking about the nature of light.

**How physics lost ether**

The history of ether in physics is the history of a correct intuition ruined by incorrect implementation.

Ether was recognized by René Descartes, Christiaan Huygens, Leonhard Euler, James Clerk Maxwell, Michael Faraday — some of the greatest physicists of their time. They felt that space could not be empty: the transmission of light and electromagnetic phenomena requires a carrier medium. They were on the right path.

But this ether had one unresolved contradiction. The polarization of light indicated that light waves are transverse — that is, oscillations occur perpendicular to the direction of propagation. In mechanics, transverse waves can propagate only in solids — in liquids and gases they decay. Therefore, for light to propagate, the ether must be solid.

But a solid ether seemed absurd. How do planets move in a solid medium? How does anything move through a solid body at all? Physicists could not overcome this psychological barrier — and abandoned ether. They preferred to declare light waves “electromagnetic,” propagating in empty space — without explaining what these waves physically are, or how exactly they move.

This was a fatal mistake. Not because a solid ether is absurd. But because physicists misunderstood what “solid” means when applied to a primary medium.

Aols are indeed extremely hard. But the hardness of aol space is fundamentally different from the hardness of matter. The hardness of matter is the strength of bonds between atoms. The hardness of aol space is the hardness of individual unbound spheres in dense contact under pressure. They are not connected to each other. There is zero friction between them — aols are perfectly smooth.

Therefore, a body moving through aol space does not encounter the resistance it would face in a solid monolith. Aols shift aside, flow around the body, and close again behind it. This is not motion through a monolith — it is motion through a densely packed but unbound medium.

It is precisely this distinction that physicists failed to understand — and they discarded ether along with both its flaws and its strengths.

**The Michelson experiment and what was being searched for**

After ether had been conceptually abandoned, Albert A. Michelson and Edward W. Morley attempted to detect it experimentally — through the so-called “ether wind.” 

The logic was as follows: if the Earth moves through ether, then in the direction of motion light should travel slightly slower, and perpendicular to it — slightly faster.
No difference was detected. 

And the final conclusion was made — there is no ether.

But in the aol model, this result is explained in a completely different way — without rejecting the medium.
The speed of transmission of vibrations in aol space is determined by the hardness of aols and the density of their packing. 

Both of these characteristics are constant and identical in all directions. 
The number of aols between any two points of the Michelson apparatus does not change depending on the orientation of the device or the speed of the Earth.

Therefore, the speed of light is the same in all directions — not because there is no ether, but because the ether is homogeneous and isotropic.

There is no ether wind and cannot be.
Michelson was looking for the wrong ether. 
And did not find it precisely because he was looking for the wrong thing.

**Why aol space is not ether**

When I wrote this hypothesis in 2000, I did not think about ether at all. Aol space was for me an independent idea — so different from the familiar concept of ether that the term itself did not even come to mind. I remember my disappointment when, finishing the work, I suddenly realized that what I was describing could technically be called ether. It irritated me for a long time.

## Chapter 42. MATTER: FROM THE PRIMARY PARTICLE TO THE UNIVERSE

There is a feature of human psychology that is rarely discussed in the context of science, yet it explains a great deal.
When we face something unknown, we almost inevitably complicate it. Darkness frightens us — and in a dark room we begin to imagine portals to another world. When the light is turned on, it turns out that the source of the eerie rustling  is just a lazy cat on the couch. But the one who ran out of the room in panic will never learn about the cat. He will carry away a story about a portal.

This is exactly what happens in physics. Faced with the unknown, a person becomes uncertain — and attributes to it the most fantastic, sometimes absurd properties. The less understandable a phenomenon is, the more mysterious and complex it appears. 

That is why we have neutrinos — particles that pass through hypothetical planets billions of kilometers thick without noticing obstacles. That is why there are virtual particles that “may or may not exist.” That is why there is a point in which all the matter of the Universe supposedly once fit.

Let us try a different approach. Let us take Occam’s razor — the principle of not multiplying entities beyond necessity — and ask ourselves simple questions about matter. Not fantasizing, but reasoning.

**Ten questions about primary matter**

**First question: what is matter made of?**

Two possibilities. Either matter is a single continuous mass occupying the entire Universe. Or it consists of tiny primary particles.  
A continuous mass is an obvious absurdity. We would be sitting inside it like insects in amber and would not be reasoning about anything.

Conclusion: **matter consists of tiny primary particles.**

**Second question: is the entire volume of the Universe filled with matter?**

Two possibilities. Either matter occupies only part of space, leaving vast empty regions. Or it fills everything.  
If there is emptiness — why? Why is matter located exactly there and in that way? What created these islands in an infinite void? There is no logical answer. Nature does not tolerate emptiness — this is not a poetic metaphor, but a mechanical principle: if matter exists, there is no reason for its absence somewhere.

Conclusion: **matter fills the entire volume of the Universe.**

**Third question: does matter possess intelligence?**

No. Matter is substance. It does not think, does not plan, does not set goals. Everything that happens to matter is the result of mechanics, not intention.

Conclusion: **matter is passive and acts only according to mechanical laws.**

**Fourth question: can primary matter be of several types?**

The existence of multiple types of primary matter would imply some kind of design — someone would have decided in advance how many types there should be and what they should be like. This leads to the idea of God. And God raises the question of where God came from — and there is no answer and cannot be one. Self-sufficient matter does not require a creator.

Conclusion: **primary matter is uniform — one type of primary particle.**

**Fifth question: what can matter do?**

Only one thing — move. This is shown by all our observations of the world.

Conclusion: **the only ability of matter is motion.**

**Sixth question: what shape are primary particles?**

Cubes and parallelepipeds would form a single monolith — flat faces fit tightly together, and motion inside such a system would be impossible. Shapeless particles lead to the same problem — a lump. Cones are too artificial.  
Spheres are the only shape that simultaneously ensures dense filling of space and the possibility of mutual movement. A sphere is the most natural geometric form without privileged directions.  
As for hardness: absolutely soft spheres would stick together into a lump. 
Absolutely rigid ones would not be able to form any structures — they would always remain separate spheres. What is needed is high, but not absolute, rigidity. **In a compressed and vibrating environment, any shape will become a sphere.**

Conclusion: **primary particles are spheres of high but not infinite rigidity.**

**Seventh question: do primary particles have complex properties — charge, magnetism, gravity?**

Why would primitive matter need such complexities? Where would charges come from in mindless substance — if no one even knows what a charge really is? Complex properties of primary particles imply a complex design. But there is no design.

Conclusion: **primary particles have no complex properties. They have only shape and rigidity.**

**Eighth question: how do atoms arise from primary particles?**

Standard physics proposes hundreds of different particles with complex properties that somehow assemble into atoms. How they assemble is unknown. There is no mechanism.  
The aol model proposes something different. Two types of elements: smooth spheres and spheres with two indentations formed under pressure. From these, under pressure and vibration, stable structures — atoms — self-organize. Without design. Without complex properties. Pure mechanics.

Conclusion: **atoms are built from one type of primary particle through deformation and self-organization under pressure.**

**Ninth question: can an empty object be solid?**

The standard atom is almost entirely empty. The nucleus occupies a negligible fraction of its volume. Electrons move somewhere at the edges. Everything else is emptiness.  
But emptiness cannot be solid. This contradicts all mechanical experience. Solidity is resistance to compression. Only something that exists can resist. Emptiness resists nothing.
If atoms are solid — they cannot be empty. If they are empty — they cannot be solid. One of the two. In the aol model, atoms are solid because they consist of solid primary particles. There is no paradox.

Conclusion: **emptiness cannot be solid and incompressible, so atoms cannot be empty.**

**Tenth question: does a mechanism of attraction exist?**

Draw two spheres at a distance. Try to imagine how one pulls the other — without contact, without a mediator, through emptiness. No such mechanism exists. In real life, we never encounter attraction — only pressure and push.

But a mechanism of compression does exist — it is the pressure of the aol medium. Bodies move closer not because they pull each other, but because the medium presses them together where the pressure is lower.

Conclusion: **attraction does not exist. What exists is compression by the pressure of the medium.**
